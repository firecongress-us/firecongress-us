### Facebook

James Madison knew that good intentions alone would not sustain a republic. When he wrote Federalist No. 10 in 1787, he described the specific way corruption enters government: politicians "of factious tempers, of local prejudices, or of sinister designs" would, "by intrigue, by corruption, or by other means, first obtain the suffrages, and then betray the interests of the people."

He was not being cynical. He was being precise. The Founders built a system of checks and balances because they understood that the people elected to run government would sometimes use it for their own benefit instead of the public's. The structure of government was one safeguard. The other was an informed citizenry that actually paid attention.
