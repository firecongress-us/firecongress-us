### Facebook

George Washington's Farewell Address, published September 19, 1796, is one of the most cited documents in American political history. Washington had watched the first organized political parties take shape during his administration and expressed deep concerns about where partisan spirit could lead.

In the address, he warned that political factions, though they might "now and then answer popular ends," were "likely, in the course of time and things, to become potent engines, by which cunning, ambitious, and unprincipled men will be enabled to subvert the power of the people and to usurp for themselves the reins of government, destroying afterwards the very engines which have lifted them to unjust dominion."

Washington stepped down after two terms rather than seek a third. The concern he put in writing in 1796, that political machinery built for public ends can be captured by those with personal ambitions, is one that every generation of Americans has had to reckon with.
