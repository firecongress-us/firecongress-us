### Facebook

Congressional incumbents won re-election at a 98% rate in 2024, according to Ballotpedia's post-election analysis. That number has hovered between 95% and 98% for most of the past decade, across multiple elections and both major parties. Even in 2010, when incumbents faced one of the most anti-Washington electorates in recent history, 85% of those who ran still kept their seats.

One reason for this consistency is money. In the 2022 Senate cycle, the average incumbent raised $29.7 million compared to $2.1 million for the average challenger, per data tracked by GovFacts. That is roughly a 14-to-1 funding gap. And the trend is long-running: in House races, the incumbent spending advantage over challengers has grown from a 3-to-2 ratio in 1972 to a 6.5-to-1 ratio in recent years. That gap exists before most voters have heard the challenger's name.
