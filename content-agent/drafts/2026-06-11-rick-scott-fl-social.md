### Facebook

**Post:**
Sen. Rick Scott (R-FL) served as CEO of Columbia/HCA, the largest for-profit hospital company in the United States, until 1997, when FBI agents raided company facilities as part of a federal fraud investigation. Scott resigned that year. Over the following six years, the company paid more than $1.7 billion in settlements to resolve what the Justice Department called "the largest healthcare fraud case in U.S. history." The company had billed Medicare for tests never ordered, falsely inflated patient records to collect higher reimbursements, and billed Medicaid for home visits that were never made.

Scott was never charged. He left the company with $9.88 million in severance and stock worth more than $350 million, according to public records.

In 2022, Scott voted against the Inflation Reduction Act, which would have allowed Medicare to negotiate lower drug prices and capped insulin costs for Medicare patients at $35 per month. In 2025, he pushed an amendment that would have added $313 billion in Medicaid cuts to an already-sweeping bill. The Senate declined to include it. Scott voted for the final bill anyway. The law, signed July 4, 2025, is projected by the CBO to cause 11.8 million Americans to lose health coverage by 2034.

Scott now chairs the Senate Special Committee on Aging, the committee with oversight responsibility for Medicare and Medicaid. Read the full accountability report at [LINK] and find out how to contact your senators at FireCongress.us/take-action.
