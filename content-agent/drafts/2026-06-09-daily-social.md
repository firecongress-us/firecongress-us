Washington's 1796 Farewell Address is remembered mostly as advice to avoid foreign entanglements, but there's a passage in it that gets quoted less often. After two terms as president, Washington wrote that political factions were "...likely, in the course of time and things, to become potent engines, by which cunning, ambitious, and unprincipled men will be enabled to subvert the power of the people."

He wasn't describing a foreign threat. He was describing what happens when political structures, over time, are captured by the people who operate them. The concern wasn't that bad actors would break into government. It was that they would work within it, use its machinery, and eventually become indistinguishable from it.
