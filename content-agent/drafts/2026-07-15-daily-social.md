### Facebook

When you hear about "outside spending" in elections, here is what that means in practice: the four super PACs most responsible for electing members of Congress (two aligned with Democrats, two aligned with Republicans) have raised nearly $492 million combined since January 2025. Of that total, $119 million (24%) came from affiliated dark money groups, meaning donors whose identities are not publicly disclosed.

Both parties use the same structure. On the Democratic side, the Senate Majority PAC raised $56 million in Q1 2026 alone. Forty-four percent of that ($25 million) came from a single dark money group called Majority Forward. On the Republican side, the Senate Leadership Fund raised $72 million in Q1 2026. About 16% came from One Nation, its affiliated dark money group. These transactions are legal. But the original donors behind those dark money groups remain unknown to voters.

This is not a partisan problem. It is a structural one. Both parties have built campaign finance operations that depend on money voters cannot trace.
