### Facebook

In 2024, 98% of congressional incumbents who ran for reelection won their seats, according to Ballotpedia's analysis of federal races. That rate has climbed steadily: 93% in 2020, 94% in 2022, and now 98%. By almost any measure, once you win a seat in Congress, you tend to keep it.

It's not just that incumbents win. They also serve for a long time. The average House member in the 119th Congress has served 8.6 years. The average senator has served 11.2 years, according to the Congressional Research Service's profile of the current Congress. In 2024, only 62 of 435 House members were newly elected, representing 14.1% of the chamber.

One structural reason behind those numbers is money. The median House incumbent raised $2.1 million during the 2024 election cycle, about $2,900 every day, according to Issue One's analysis of FEC filings. The median senator seeking reelection raised $11.1 million. Challengers rarely enter a race with the donor networks, committee assignments, or name recognition that produce fundraising at that scale. The system tends to reward tenure, which tends to produce more tenure. You can look up your own representatives and see how long they've served at [LINK].
