### Facebook

In the 2024 elections, 98% of congressional incumbents who ran for reelection won. In 41 states, not a single sitting member of Congress lost their seat. This was not a Democratic or Republican sweep. It was the status quo holding, as it almost always does.

The numbers inside the 119th Congress tell the same story. The average House member has served 9.9 years. The average senator has been in office 16.2 years. Just 75 of the 435 House seats are held by freshmen. The institution that Americans consistently rate as one of the least trusted in the country is also one of the hardest to change from the outside.

These patterns affect voters in both parties. When incumbents rarely lose, primaries and general elections become less competitive, and the donors and PACs who fund campaigns can predict with near-certainty who will still be in office next cycle.
