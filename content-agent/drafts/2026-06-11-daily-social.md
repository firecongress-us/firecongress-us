### Facebook

In the 2025-2026 election cycle, the four super PACs focused on winning control of the House and Senate have raised a combined $492 million. About $119 million of that, roughly 1 in 4 dollars, came from affiliated dark money groups: organizations that can donate unlimited amounts to super PACs without being required to publicly disclose their own donors. That figure comes from a 2026 analysis by Issue One, a nonpartisan campaign finance watchdog.

This is not a one-party pattern. The two Democratic congressional super PACs, Senate Majority PAC and House Majority PAC, draw 29% and 17% of their funding from anonymous dark money sources, respectively. The two Republican super PACs, Senate Leadership Fund and Congressional Leadership Fund, draw 26% and 22%. All four operate within the same legal structure, and all four benefit from the same disclosure gap.

When a significant share of the money that decides who controls Congress cannot be traced to a named donor, it becomes harder for voters to know whose interests their representatives are actually serving.
