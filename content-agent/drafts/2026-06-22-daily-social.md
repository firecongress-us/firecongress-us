### Facebook

On September 17, 1796, George Washington published his Farewell Address. He was 64 years old and stepping away from the presidency he could have held indefinitely. In it, he warned that political factions were likely to become "potent engines, by which cunning, ambitious, and unprincipled men will be enabled to subvert the power of the people and to usurp for themselves the reins of government."

Washington wasn't predicting which party would cause the most damage. He was describing something structural: what happens when the people who benefit from political power are also the ones who control how long they get to keep it. His concern wasn't partisanship. It was the absence of guardrails.

Two centuries later, that absence is still the issue. Incumbents in both parties retain enormous structural advantages in fundraising, name recognition, and committee access that make them difficult to unseat regardless of performance.
