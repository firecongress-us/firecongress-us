### Facebook

Rep. Tim Walberg of Michigan has served in Congress since 2011. In June 2026, he filed his first-ever congressional stock disclosure, revealing purchases made in early February 2025 of Chevron, ExxonMobil, Boeing, Lockheed Martin, L3Harris Technologies, and other companies. Federal law requires members of Congress to disclose stock trades within 45 days. Walberg's disclosure came more than 14 months past that deadline.

At the time of the purchases, Walberg served on the House Committee on Natural Resources, which oversees petroleum conservation on federal lands. The STOCK Act's disclosure rules are designed to let the public identify whether a lawmaker's financial holdings overlap with their policy decisions. Walberg's spokesperson said a financial adviser made the trades without notifying him. The standard penalty for a late STOCK Act disclosure is a $200 fine.

Walberg's League of Conservation Voters score for 2025 was 0%, meaning he cast no pro-environment votes on the measures LCV tracked that year. He voted to roll back the federal methane pollution fee, block cleaner truck standards, and gut Clean Air Act protections. He also voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which repealed the $7,500 federal EV tax credit and reverted oil royalty rates on federal lands.
