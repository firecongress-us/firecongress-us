In the 2026 midterm primaries, voters across the country are seeing ads paid for by front groups with generic-sounding names. A May 2026 investigation by The Intercept tracked dozens of these groups spending millions before primary elections. By the time federal filings reveal who actually funded the ads, the vote is already cast.

This is not a Republican or Democratic problem. It is a structural one. The cryptocurrency industry, for example, funds Protect Progress, which spent over $4 million supporting a candidate in a Texas congressional runoff, and also funds Defend American Jobs on the Republican side. The AI industry operates the same way, with affiliated groups aligned with both parties. Spending is timed to fall just before disclosure deadlines, so voters see the ads without seeing who paid for them.

Campaign finance expert Shanna Ports described the effect plainly: front groups' spending before primaries is "anonymous to voters," especially when those groups use names that carry no meaning to the average person.
