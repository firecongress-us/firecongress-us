### Facebook

George Washington's 1796 Farewell Address is best known for its warning against foreign entanglements. Less often quoted is what Washington said about political parties.

In that same document, Washington warned that parties could become "potent engines, by which cunning, ambitious, and unprincipled men will be enabled to subvert the power of the people, and to usurp for themselves the reins of government." He called it "the interest and duty of a wise people to discourage and restrain" the spirit of party, and warned against its "baneful effects" on popular government.

Washington wrote this after watching the Federalist and Democratic-Republican parties emerge during his own presidency. His concern was not abstract. It was based on what he observed in real time.
