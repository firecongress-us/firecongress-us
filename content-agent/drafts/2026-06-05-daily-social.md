### Facebook

In the first quarter of 2026, the four main super PACs fighting for control of Congress collected nearly $48 million from affiliated dark money groups. Since January 2025, those four committees have raised $492 million combined, and about $119 million of it (24%) traces back to groups that are not required to disclose where their money comes from. That figure comes from Issue One, a nonpartisan campaign finance watchdog that analyzed Q1 2026 fundraising filings.

The pattern cuts across both parties. The Senate Majority PAC, which supports Democratic Senate candidates, got 29% of its $116 million total from a dark money affiliate. The Senate Leadership Fund, which supports Republican Senate candidates, got 26% of its $175 million from a dark money group. The Congressional Leadership Fund (R) came in at 22% and the House Majority PAC (D) at 17%. In all four cases, the underlying donors funding those affiliated nonprofits are never publicly disclosed.

Dark money contributions are legal. They flow through 501(c)(4) nonprofits, which can spend on elections without revealing their backers.
