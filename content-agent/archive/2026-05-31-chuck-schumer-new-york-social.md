### Facebook

**Post:**

Sen. Chuck Schumer (D-NY) has served in the Senate since 1999 and led Senate Democrats since 2017. He has also received more from Wall Street's securities and investment industry than nearly any other member of Congress — over $10.4 million since 1989, with Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, and JPMorgan Chase as his three largest career donors.

In March 2025, Schumer voted with Senate Republicans to pass a stopgap spending bill that cut nondefense domestic spending by $3.1 billion while boosting defense by $7 billion. The National Low Income Housing Coalition documented the consequences: 32,000 families projected to lose housing vouchers, a $3.29 billion cut to HUD's Community Development Fund, and a $168 million shortfall in homeless assistance programs. Schumer called the bill "a bad bill" before voting for it, saying a shutdown would be worse.

Later that year, a 43-day government shutdown ended without the ACA subsidy extension Democrats had demanded — leaving 24 million Americans facing higher health insurance premiums. Seven Democrats broke ranks, and Schumer, though he did not vote for the final deal, faced calls from his own party to resign as minority leader. He remained in place.
