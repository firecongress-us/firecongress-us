House Majority Leader Steve Scalise, who represents Louisiana's 1st Congressional District, collected $694,124 from oil and gas interests across the 2022 and 2023-24 election cycles, placing him among the top four House recipients of fossil fuel donations. During that same period, he introduced H.R. 1, the Lower Energy Costs Act, which reduced the royalty rates oil producers pay on federal leases, and he served as the lead floor strategist who secured the votes to pass the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, a 2025 law that expanded offshore drilling rights and eliminated most federal clean energy tax credits.

## Major Donors

According to data cited by E&E News, Scalise received $368,291 from the oil and gas industry in the 2022 election cycle. In the following cycle, Common Dreams reported his oil and gas receipts totaled $325,833, bringing his two-cycle total to $694,124. His top political action committee donors include Koch Industries and Valero Energy, two of the largest domestic petroleum companies. The American Petroleum Institute contributed $5,000 to his campaign in 2023, and a Koch Industries lobbyist, Brian Henneberry, contributed $30,000 to the Scalise Leadership Fund that same year.

Scalise also received $40,000 from the Williams Companies PAC. The board of Williams Companies includes executives from Pioneer Natural Resources, whose former CEO the Federal Trade Commission accused in 2024 of colluding with OPEC to limit oil production and inflate consumer prices. Scalise ranked fourth among all House members in oil and gas receipts during the 2023-24 cycle.

## Bills That Aligned With Donor Interests

In March 2023, Scalise introduced H.R. 1, the Lower Energy Costs Act. The bill passed the House 225 to 204 on March 30, 2023. Its provisions reduced the royalty rates that oil, gas, and coal producers pay on federal land leases, eased permitting requirements for pipelines and refineries, and reinstated noncompetitive leasing of public lands, which allows energy companies to acquire leases without a competitive bidding process. Representative Raul Grijalva, the top Democrat on the House Natural Resources Committee, described the bill as a "nearly 200-page love letter to polluting industries." The Senate did not take up the measure.

Two years later, Scalise played a more consequential role on a bill that did become law. As House Majority Leader, he managed floor strategy for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act during the final hours before passage. The Shreveport Bossier Advocate reported that Scalise worked directly with holdout members on the House floor, converting opposition into support. He delivered the closing pitch to Republican members before the bill cleared the House 218 to 214 on July 3, 2025. President Trump signed it into law on July 4, 2025.

The law requires a minimum of 30 offshore oil and gas lease sales in the Gulf of America over 15 years, mandates lease sales in the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge, and reverts federal onshore royalty rates to the levels that were in place before the Inflation Reduction Act took effect in 2022. At the same time, the law terminated the federal consumer tax credit for electric vehicles, effective September 30, 2025, ended tax credits for new commercial clean vehicles, and phased out federal tax credits for wind and solar projects beginning construction more than 12 months after enactment.

## What This Costs Constituents

The Center for American Progress projected the bill's effects on Louisiana before it became law. The organization estimated that Louisiana households would pay $121 more per year in electricity costs and $138 more per year in gasoline by 2035. The analysis projected that $40.1 billion in planned clean energy investments in Louisiana (concentrated in hydrogen and solar) would be at risk, along with 26,940 Louisiana jobs tied to those projects.

At the national level, the Rhodium Group estimated the law would reduce new clean energy capacity additions by 57 to 62 percent between 2025 and 2035, raise household energy expenditures by $78 to $192 per year by 2035, and put $522 billion in announced clean energy investments at risk. A Newsweek report citing analysis from Energy Innovation projected 760,000 clean energy sector jobs nationally would be lost by 2030 as a result of the law's clean energy credit terminations.
