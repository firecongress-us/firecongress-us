In the 2024 general election, the crypto-industry-funded super PAC Protect Progress spent **$10,039,655** to support Ruben Gallego's Arizona Senate campaign. After he took office, Gallego became the top Democrat on the Senate Banking Committee's digital assets subcommittee. From that seat he helped negotiate the GENIUS Act, a federal stablecoin law, and voted for it. Separately, he disclosed two personal investments years after the STOCK Act deadline, and in 2026 he faced reporting about campaign and PAC money spent on family trips and Super Bowl tickets.

## Who backed him

Gallego was one of the top recipients of crypto industry campaign support in 2024. In August 2024, NBC News reported that Fairshake PAC and its affiliated super PACs had committed about $3 million to support him against Republican Kari Lake. The final total was much higher. The Follow the Crypto tracker reports that Protect Progress spent $10,039,655 supporting Gallego in the general election. Sludge, citing OpenSecrets, also puts the figure at $10 million. This was outside spending by an independent super PAC, not direct contributions to his campaign.

After the election, fundraising ties to the industry continued. Sludge reported that earlier in 2025 Gallego headlined a fundraiser with Marc Andreessen, co-founder of the venture firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z). In August 2025, Gallego held a Nantucket fundraiser co-hosted by Mike Smith of Cornerstone Government Affairs. According to lobbying disclosures cited by Sludge, a16z pays Smith and Cornerstone $80,000 a quarter to lobby Congress on cryptocurrency and other issues. Cornerstone also lobbies for the stablecoin issuer Circle and the payments company Stripe.

## Votes and actions on crypto legislation

As ranking member of the Digital Assets Subcommittee, Gallego led a group of Democratic negotiators on the GENIUS Act, a bill setting rules for dollar-backed stablecoins.

- **May 8, 2025:** A cloture vote on the bill failed 48-49. Gallego voted no along with every other Democrat and three Republicans. He told Politico, as quoted by Sludge, "We need time" and "We're not shutting down."
- **May 19, 2025:** Gallego announced he would vote to advance the bill, saying negotiations were "headed in a direction that addresses many of the concerns my colleagues and I have raised." The statement did not identify which provisions changed.
- **June 17, 2025:** The Senate passed the GENIUS Act 68-30. Gallego was one of 18 Democrats who voted yes. The House passed it in July, and President Trump signed it on July 18, 2025.

Gallego then took a leading role on the larger crypto market structure bill, known as the CLARITY Act. On May 14, 2026, the Banking Committee advanced the bill 15-9. Gallego and Sen. Angela Alsobrooks (D-MD) were the only Democrats to vote yes. Gallego said at the time that "my vote here does not guarantee a vote on the floor," and that negotiators had not yet agreed on an ethics guardrail for elected officials.

On September 15, 2026, a cloture vote on the CLARITY Act failed 49-50. Gallego voted no. American Banker reported that he led Democrats' ethics negotiations, and he said he would not "support any piece of legislation that enables" President Trump's crypto profits. "The legislation failed squarely because Republicans refuse to say no to the president," Gallego said.

So the record runs both ways. Gallego voted for the stablecoin law the industry sought, and he backed the market structure bill in committee. On the Senate floor, he voted against advancing it when the ethics provision he wanted wasn't included.

## Disclosure and spending questions

In August 2024, Raw Story reported that Gallego, then a House member, disclosed two purchases years after the STOCK Act's 45-day deadline. The purchases were a stake in Aspiration Fund Adviser LLC bought August 27, 2019, and NameCoach Inc. securities bought in June 2022, each valued at $15,001 to $50,000. Raw Story reported that the Aspiration Fund stake was filed as a late periodic transaction report on August 13, 2024, and the NameCoach investment was disclosed in an amended annual report filed the same day. A spokesperson said the investments "were disclosed in previous filings and the recently filed report corrects inadvertent errors."

In June 2026, Politico reported, and KJZZ summarized, that Gallego's leadership PAC, JUNTOS PAC, spent about $1,500 on travel, lodging, and meals at Disneyland and $2,900 at Disney World. California Globe, citing Politico, reported that a joint fundraising committee Gallego shared with former Rep. Eric Swalwell spent $34,700 on tickets to the 2023 Super Bowl. KJZZ reported that Gallego, Swalwell, their families, and several donors attended the 2023 Super Bowl as a fundraising event. Gallego wrote that using campaign funds for "fundraising costs, travel, and offset the cost of childcare is well within the rules." He wrote, "I'm not a millionaire, and I have a blended family, so I don't have that option."

## What this means for constituents

Stablecoins regulated under the GENIUS Act are not bank deposits, so they carry no FDIC insurance. Consumer Reports said the Senate passed the bill "without safeguards needed to protect consumers." New York Attorney General Letitia James and other prosecutors argued that the law does not require issuers to return stolen funds to fraud victims. Arizonans who hold or use stablecoins are covered by those rules. Gallego helped write them after a crypto-industry-funded super PAC spent more than $10 million to elect him.
